2026 Transit Lockout

Updated November 22, 2026

  • Call to Action for Regina Residents: Talking points, social media post suggestions, and letter writing tips.

Quick facts

  • It's a "lockout," not a "strike": The City responded to the bus drivers' work-to-rule measure of pausing voluntary overtime by locking them out. Calling it a strike adopts the City's framing.
  • Issues on the table: pay that has fallen behind the cost of living, benefits, and driver safety, according to ATU Canada president John Di Nino and earlier union statements.
  • What still runs: as of Mon Nov 23, 2026, paratransit buses are the only buses running.
  • Who is hurt the most: low-wage workers, newcomers, students, people with disabilities and people without housing. See the demographics of transit riders.
  • Record breaking year: Regina Transit carried nearly 9 million trips in 2025(CTV)

How the City escalated contract negotiations

The union took the smallest step available; the City took the largest. That gap is the core of the case.

ATU Local 588City of Regina
ActionStop working voluntary overtimeCancel every regular route, then lock workers out
Effect on regular shiftsEvery regular shift still workedNo shifts worked; workers unpaid after 11AM Monday
Effect on ridersSome trips at risk if they depended on overtimeNo buses at all, with no end date
What it signalsPressure to settlePressure on workers by cutting off riders
  1. Other cities ran reduced service instead. When Saskatoon's transit union refused overtime in 2016, the city changed routes and kept buses moving (CJME). Lethbridge cancelled some routes during a no-overtime action rather than shutting down (CTV).
  2. The City handled job actions better in the past. In 2013, the union ran weeks of escalating action, including drivers going without uniforms and driving below the speed limit. The City issued a lockout notice, but buses kept running, and a deal was reached (Global News, Global News).
  3. Needing overtime to run means too few drivers. The City's own 2018 budget described transit as "consistently understaffed" with rising overtime costs (StarPhoenix). In 2026 the deputy city manager said nearly all routes were already at capacity (Leader-Post via Yahoo). Riders should not pay for a staffing gap by losing all service.
  4. The public was kept in the dark. The City's notice cited an "operational safety directive", but workers shared a letter online where the City calls it a lockout. (CJME).
  5. The timing makes it worse. The shutdown starts on the opening day of Agribition, the city's largest annual event. Visitors will no longer be eligible for free bus rides to the venue and discounted entry fees and limited parking at REAL will get worse.
  6. It fits a pattern. City council continues to show that public transit is a low priority. Riders saw 16 proposed transit cuts in December 2025 and then a 10% fare increase in April 2026. City Council passed the fare increase over objections from students, school trustees, and public transit advocates. Bus drivers' wages, safety, and well-being are important to a thriving public transit system, but City Council continues to erode it.
  7. Lockouts could backfire on the City. Ever since the proposed transit cuts and route reductions in the December 2025 budget, public trust in City Council has eroded. This lockout action is only solidifying public opinion and forcing residents to rally to the cause.

Why transit matters in Regina

Transit is growing, and the City's own plans depend on it. Shutting it down sets back years of work and investment.

  • Record demand. Ridership rose from 7.37 million trips in 2019 to over 7.5 million in 2023 and nearly 9 million in 2025 (620 CKRM, CTV).
  • The City's own goal. Council's 2022 Transit Master Plan aims to make transit "a main transportation mode of choice" over 25 years (City of Regina).
  • Riders already give up a lot. Bus commutes average 31.5 minutes, against 16.3 by car. 28% of bus commuters travel 45 minutes or more, against 4% of drivers (2021 Census, YQR Transit analysis).
  • Getting kids to school. When Johnson Collegiate students rode free in fall 2025, ridership tripled compared with the months after (Leader-Post via Yahoo). Students want to ride; now they can't.
  • Getting to hospital. Route 2 is the only route stopping within 300 m of the General Hospital (Regina Transit GTFS, YQR Transit analysis).
  • Public money sitting idle. The City bought 20 battery-electric buses, which it says cost $0.76 a kilometre to run against $1.51 for diesel (City of Regina). Taxpayers fund the fleet whether or not it runs.
  • A cold-weather lifeline. Buses are warm places to wait and travel, and the City has used them as overnight warming spaces in past cold snaps.
