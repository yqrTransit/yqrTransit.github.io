Who is hurt most by the 2026 Transit Lockout

The people impacted by the Transit lockout are the ones with the fewest other options: low-wage workers, newcomers, students, people with disabilities and people without housing. Regina Transit carried nearly 9 million trips in 2025, its highest year on record (CTV).

WhoWhy the lockout hits hardSource
Low-wage workersTransit commuters' median employment income is $22,600. Meanwhile, Regina residents who drive to work have a median employment income of $51,600. For low-wage workers, missing shifts because of a lack of transportation can financially devastate those who already earn so little. 2021 Census, YQR Transit analysis
Newcomers and international students23% of non-permanent residents and 10% of recent immigrants in Regina (2016–21) commute by transit compared to 1.7% of people born in Canada.2021 Census, YQR Transit analysis
High school studentsAbout half of students at Regina's seven public high schools depend on transit. Regina Catholic Schools students rely on City transit as the division runs no buses for high schoolers. Additionally, special routes that charter students to their high school are cancelled as well.SaskToday, Regina Catholic Schools
University studentsU of R students pay a U-Pass fee each semester for unlimited transit, but they lose it for the last weeks of the fall term.U of R
People with disabilitiesAs Paratransit is the only service left, its buses will become more overburdened. Many disabled bus riders who normally use regular routes can't switch over to paratransit services.City notice
People without housing1,156 people were counted as homeless in Regina in 2026, and shelters remain full. In past cold snaps, the City used a Regina Transit bus as an overnight warming space, most recently Jan 22–26, 2026.Eagle Feather News, City of Regina, Canadian Centre for Policy Alternatives
Health and care support workers, retail and service workersThese are the riders seen most often at hospitals, urgent care, medical diagnostic labs, care homes, and stores, often on early, late and weekend shifts.Rider observations

Neighbourhoods most dependent on the bus: Heritage, Gladmer Park, Albert Park, North Central, Hillsdale and Centre Square rank highest on our transit-need index (income, renters, newcomers, Indigenous identity, age 15–24, transit use). Albert Park has the highest transit commute share of any sizable neighbourhood, 12.4%.

Note: The census figures come from May 2021, during the pandemic and most likely undercount transit ridership.

Source: 2021 StatsCan Census and the City of Regina neighbourhood profiles: yqrTransit Demographics GitHub Repo
