2026 Transit Lockout Call to Action

Keep your statements accurate

One wrong fact gives the City an easy way to dismiss everything else. Check posts and letters against these rules.

  • Say lockout, not strike: the City responded to the bus drivers' work-to-rule measure of pausing voluntary overtime by locked them out. Calling it a strike adopts the City's framing.
  • Don't call the lockout illegal: You may perceive the lockout as unfair, but it is not illegal.
  • Paratransit still runs: Say "all regular bus routes," not "all transit."
  • Trips ≠ Riders As one person can take many trips, "9 million trips" is not the same as "9 million riders."
  • 2021 Census numbers undercount transit ridership. The 2021 Census only covered work commutes in May 2021, during the pandemic.
  • No wage figures or offers: Currently neither side has made its proposals public.
  • Quote only what was said on the record, with the source named.
  • Criticize decisions, not people: Keep it about council's and administration's choices, not individual staff.

What the City says, and how to respond

Know the City's case before you argue against it. Some of it is reasonable, and saying so makes your points more credible.

The City saysA fair response
Without voluntary overtime, it "cannot maintain the minimum staffing" for safe operation, maintenance and oversight.Publish how many shifts depend on overtime. Run what regular shifts can cover, and hire to close the gap.
Resident safety comes first, and transit must be reliable so no one waits outside in cold weather.No service at all leaves everyone outside or walking and is a greater risk than reduced service. A published reduced schedule is the safest option for the circumstances.
The contract must balance staff needs with residents' and taxpayers' interests.Residents are the ones paying for this lockout, but they have lost service they already paid for in fares and taxes.
The City will not discuss bargaining "out of respect for the bargaining process."Keeping offers private is normal in the bargaining process, but no bus service is a public concern.

For social media

Rule of One in Three

Keep every post to

  • one point
  • one fact
  • one ask
A personal story ("I can't get to my class at the university") travels further than any statistic.

Core points

  1. The City overreacted:Drivers said no to voluntary overtime, but the City said no to all buses. This is a choice the city made and was not forced to make.
  2. Indications of understaffing:An overtime ban means drivers still work every regular shift and may just mean reduced service. If the system can't run without overtime, then it's understaffed.
  3. 25000 destinations with no way to get there:Regina riders took nearly 9 million trips in 2025, or roughly 25,000 trips on an average day. Each of those riders is now on their own as they head into the coldest months of the year.
  4. "All I wanna say is that they don't really care about us": The people stranded by the lockout are students, low-wage workers, newcomers, people with disabilities and people without housing. Most of these people cannot just drive instead.
  5. Expensive fares for no ridesRiders paid 10% more in fares this April, and there are indications that fares are going up even more. Now bus riders get no service at all for bus passes they have already paid for.
  6. Two years with no deal: The bus drivers have gone almost two years without a contract, and locking them out doesn't get a deal any closer.
  7. The City says this is about safety in the cold. Leaving thousands of people with no bus at all is not the safer option.
  8. Council can fix this: Direct the City to lift the lockout, restore bus service, and return to the bargaining table.

Make it count

  • Tag the mayor and your own ward councillor; name your ward and route.
  • Post photos of empty stops and of how you're getting around instead.
  • Share the City's contact information so others can write too.
  • Keep your correspondence about the lockout decision and not about any individual City staff member.

Writing your councillor and the mayor

A short, personal letter from someone in the ward, with one clear ask, carries more weight than a long one. Write to your own councillor and copy the mayor.

Contacts

  • Mayor Chad Bachynski: mayor@regina.ca, 306-777-7339 (City of Regina)
  • Councillors: email and phone are on each ward page (City Council). Find your ward with the ward map.
  • To speak at a council meeting, register at Regina.ca/Register. The 2027 budget debate runs Dec 7–11, 2026.

Ideas: What to ask for

  • Direct the City to lift the lockout and restore regular service while bargaining continues. You can use the example that Saskatoon's council voted to withdraw a transit lockout notice in 2014.
  • If full service truly can't run without overtime, then run a reduced schedule on regular shifts. Reduced service should prioritize high school and university routes, and routes serving hospitals first.
  • Get back to the table and settle a contract that's almost two years overdue. Hiring a mediator will help.
  • Tell residents how many shifts a week depend on voluntary overtime, and what the hiring plan is.
  • Refund or credit monthly passes, U-Pass fees, and affordability passes for every day without service.
  • Explain how the City will provide cold-weather help, such as the warming bus, during a lockout.
  • In the 2027 budget, fund enough operators that service never depends on overtime.

Points to make in a letter

Don't use a form letter because those can go to email spam folders.

  • Use a clear subject that directly states the desired action. For example, "Restore Regina Transit service now"
  • State your neighbourhood and ward in the email
  • State what bus route you and your family uses and frequent destinations by bus.
  • State how you, your family, or employees will be affected by the lockout in one or two sentences. If you are not personally affected, then mention that it will be most difficult for students, low-wage workers, people with disabilities and people without housing.
  • Frame the facts. For example, bus drivers only stopped volunteering overtime, but the City chose to shut down all service instead of reducing the schedule.
  • State your one ask. For example, asking for a reduced schedule instead of a full shutdown of service.
  • Include your full name, phone number, and address. Ward councillors want to know that you reside in their ward.
